## 🧮 Simulation Project: Loan Bias & Proxy Variables (30% weighting)  

- **Files:**  
  - [ST313_simulation_48626.Rmd](https://github.com/shuja-ali298/Uni-Projects/blob/main/ST313%3A%20Ethics%20in%20Data%20Science/ST313_simulation_48626.Rmd)  
  - [ST313_simulation_48626.html](https://github.com/shuja-ali298/Uni-Projects/blob/main/ST313%3A%20Ethics%20in%20Data%20Science/ST313_simulation_48626.html)  

**Overview:**  
This simulation investigated how **racial bias** can arise in **loan predictions**, even when race itself is not directly included as a variable. By illustrating the concepts of **downstream variables** and **proxies**, it demonstrated how biased structures can be embedded in training data, silently reproducing discrimination.  

**Key Elements:**  
- Generated synthetic loan applicant data.  
- Explored how variables such as income, postcode, or repayment history can act as **proxies for race**.  
- Showed how model predictions can become **race-sensitive** even without race as an explicit input.  
- Discussed ethical implications of proxy variables and accountability in algorithmic decision-making.  

**Result:** ✅ *90/100*

## ✍️ Essay Project: Quilliam’s Grooming Gangs Study (70% weighting)  

- **File:** [ST313_essay_shuja_ali.pdf](https://github.com/shuja-ali298/Uni-Projects/blob/main/ST313%3A%20Ethics%20in%20Data%20Science/ST313_essay_shuja_ali.pdf)  

**Title:** *Systemic Bias in Data Science: Quilliam’s Grooming Gangs Case Study*  

**Overview:**  
This essay critiqued the **2017 Quilliam Foundation study**, which claimed 84% of “grooming gang” offenders in the UK were of Asian heritage. Despite widespread media coverage, the study was **methodologically flawed and ethically irresponsible**.  

**Main Critiques:**  
- **Selective variable definition:** Quilliam introduced the vague label “grooming gangs,” excluding white-dominated online offenders to inflate disproportionality.  
- **Opacity & non-replicability:** Their “extensive data mining” was undocumented, making findings unverifiable.  
- **Flawed causal reasoning:** Correlation was conflated with causation by linking offences to cultural/religious practices.  
- **Ethical framing:** Illustrated a “shoe-clerk” mentality (Bross, 1974), prioritising sensational results over methodological rigour.  

**Ethical Analysis:**  
- Applied **value theory** (intrinsic vs. instrumental values).  
- Used **Belmont–Menlo principles** (respect, fairness, accountability, transparency).  
- Linked failures to the **replication crisis** in data science.  

**Conclusion:**  
The essay showed how Quilliam’s work used data to push a divisive narrative, violating principles of fairness and replicability. It warned that such practices risk being replicated in algorithmic contexts (e.g., predictive policing), where selective variable definitions embed systemic bias from the start.  

**Result:** ✅ *65/100*
